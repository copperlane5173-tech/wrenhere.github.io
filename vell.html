Vell

a small, regular language, made to be easy to learn and a little strange to say

Vell is a constructed language I built as practice against the Speedlang Challenge 28 prompt. It's small on purpose: four vowels, eleven consonants, a handful of affixes, and a pronoun set that has to be juggled a little. The character I wanted is a language someone could pick up in an afternoon and still find odd to say out loud.

Sound

Four vowels, written i, u, a, o. The o is back and open, [ɔ].

Eleven consonants. There's one nasal only, n, and it's alveolar. No m, no w, no ŋ. The system leans on fricatives.

The one rule that gives it its feel: at the end of a word, the stops soften into their fricatives. p becomes f, t becomes s, k becomes h. So top "head" is said [tof], and nak "to come" is said [nah]. Inside a word the stops stay hard. It's the plosive-to-fricative alternation the challenge asked for, and it's what makes the edges of Vell go quiet.

Building words

Vell is suffixing. A few affixes do the work:

  • -a — plural
  • -is — past
  • -vel — agent, "one who does X"
  • -n — dual, for two

Word order is subject-verb-object, and the verb doesn't change for its subject. To say no, put no before the verb. To ask a yes-no question, put ha at the front of the sentence. Possession uses vel after the possessor, and a relative clause is introduced by sa after the noun.

Pronouns

Three person roots, mi, ti, vi, take the number suffixes. That's nine distinct forms, which is why the set has a little weight to it.

Sentences

  • ti nol kora. You eat the stone.
  • ti no nol kora. You do not eat the stone.
  • ha ti nol kora? Do you eat the stone?
  • ti nol-is kora. You ate the stone.
  • kora sa ti nol-is. The stone that you ate.
  • mi vel kora. My stone.
  • ti vel tave-vel. You are a maker.
  • vi-a tav sai. They make water.

A short dialogue

Two people, one of them new to the place. The new one has been walking a while and is thirsty.

ti nak.
ha ti nol-is kora?
mi no nol-is kora. mi nol-is sai.
ha ti siv fala?
mi siv fala. fala tav sai.
vi-a vel tave-vel.
mi vel tave-vel.

You came. Did you eat the stone? I did not eat the stone. I ate the water. Did you see the fire? I saw the fire. The fire made the water. They are makers. I am a maker.

A short poem

mi nol kora
ti siv sai
vi-a tav fala

kora vel kora
sai vel sai
fala vel fala

ha ti nak-is?
mi nak-is.
ti nak-is.
vi-a nak-is.

The last stanza is a question and three answers: did you come? I came, you came, they came. Spoken, nak-is ends with the softened [h], so the poem goes out quiet.
